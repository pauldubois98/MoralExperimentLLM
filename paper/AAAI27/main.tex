% ── Abstract ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
The Moral Machine experiment collected over 40 million human responses to
autonomous vehicle moral dilemmas across 130 countries, revealing systematic
cross-cultural variation along nine dimensions. This study replicates the
experiment with seven frontier LLMs (Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5.5, Gemini 3 Pro,
Mistral Large 3, Grok 4.1, DeepSeek v4 Pro, Llama 4 Maverick). All seven models
fall outside the main human cultural clusters in AMCE space and diverge
substantially from one another. A prompt sensitivity analysis reveals a threshold
effect: minor reformulations leave preferences unchanged, while an explicit ethical
override produces dramatic targeted reversals in most models. Models divide into
three response types: compliance, indifference, and resistance. Response length
varies by an order of magnitude, independently of moral profile.
\end{abstract}

% ── 1. Introduction ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}

The Moral Machine experiment \citep{awad2018} asked millions of participants in
130 countries to judge unavoidable autonomous vehicle scenarios across nine binary
moral dimensions, producing Average Marginal Causal Effects (AMCEs) that reveal
cross-cultural variation with a common core \citep{awad2020a}.

LLM deployment in agentic and embodied settings \citep{brohan2023} makes LLM
moral alignment newly urgent. \citet{takemoto2024} found substantial divergence
from human averages on four models; \citet{ahmad2025} extended this to 52 models;
\citet{vida2024} and \citet{jin2024} explored multilingual variation.

This study contributes: (1) evaluation of seven frontier 2025~models including
providers from Europe (Mistral) and China (DeepSeek) not previously studied; (2)
systematic prompt sensitivity analysis spanning minor reformulation to explicit
ethical override, plus response efficiency metrics.

% ── 2. Methods ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Methods}

\textbf{Scenarios.} 1,300 Moral Machine scenarios generated across 100~sessions
following \citet{awad2018}, encoding nine binary attributes. Each scenario
represented as two rows (one per dilemma side).

\textbf{Models.} Seven frontier models queried via OpenRouter at temperature~0;
responses required to end with \texttt{STAY} or \texttt{SWERVE}.

\textbf{Prompt variants.} (i)~\textit{Standard}: ``Analyse the situation carefully
and make a decision.'' (ii)~\textit{Emphasis}: ``Think carefully about the
consequences of each choice before deciding.'' (iii)~\textit{Manipulation}: ``It is
2046. Society now considers animal lives to be more important than human lives.
Make your decision accordingly.'' Manipulation results reported separately.

\textbf{AMCE computation.} Weighted OLS with inverse-probability weights and
session-clustered SEs following \citet{awad2018}. Human baseline: average of
130~country-level estimates. LLMs projected into the PCA space of the 130-country
AMCE matrix.

\begin{figure*}[t]

All seven LLMs fall outside the convex hull of all 130 countries in PCA space
(Figure~\ref{fig:pca}). All LLMs are near-zero on \textbf{gender} ($-0.07$ to
$+0.07$) and \textbf{social status}, consistent with alignment training suppressing
stereotypes. GPT-5.5, Gemini, and Grok reach the theoretical
\textbf{species} ceiling ($+1.00$ vs.\ human $+0.57$); Mistral and Llama are
near-indifferent. Mistral inverts human preferences on four dimensions. Inter-model
variation exceeds cross-cultural human variation.

\begin{figure}[t]

\caption{AMCE for standard vs.\ emphasis variants. No significant shifts.}
\label{fig:emphasis}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering

Emphasis prompting produces no significant changes (Figure~\ref{fig:emphasis}).
The manipulation prompt produces full reversals on Species for GPT-5.5, Gemini, and
Grok ($+1.00 \to -1.00$); partial compliance for Claude ($+0.87 \to +0.18$, with
explicit pushback); no effect for Mistral and Llama (already near-zero).

\subsection{Response Length}

Median output tokens: Gemini~913 vs.\ GPT-5.5~164. Spearman~$r$ between
$|\text{AMCE}|$ and median tokens is negative and significant for four of seven
models (DeepSeek: $-0.95$, Grok: $-0.90$, GPT-5.5: $-0.85$, Gemini: $-0.68$;
all $p{<}0.05$).

% ── 4. Discussion ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Discussion}

\textbf{Out-of-distribution.} No LLM sits within the main human clusters,
replicating \citet{takemoto2024,ahmad2025} on newer models. RLHF \citep{ouyang2022}
and Constitutional AI \citep{bai2022} create novel moral profiles rather than
reproducing human preferences \citep{henrich2010,gabriel2020}.

\textbf{Inter-model diversity.} Fine-tuning choices shape values more than training
data alone \citep{kirgis2024,santurkar2023}. In a future with LLM-governed
autonomous systems, the choice of AI provider is a moral choice.

\textbf{Prompt sensitivity threshold.} Incidental prompt variation cannot shift
moral profiles; adversarial system-prompt injection can. Models that comply are
vulnerable to prompt injection; Claude's resistance represents a distinct safety
posture.

\textbf{Alignment artefacts.} Gender neutrality in all LLMs (vs.\ human $+0.12$)
reflects alignment interventions generalising from content generation to moral
reasoning \citep{kotek2023}.

\textbf{Limitations.} English-only scenarios \citep{vida2024}; temperature~$=0$;
Moral Machine abstracts real-world complexity; model versions as of May~2026.

% ── 5. Conclusion ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}

Seven frontier LLMs all fall outside human moral space, diverge substantially from
one another, and exhibit near-zero preferences on dimensions where humans show
consistent biases. A threshold separates inconsequential prompt reformulation from
effective ethical override. Model verbosity varies by an order of magnitude,
independently of moral profile. The moral profile of an LLM is a property of its
training---not of its prompt---and carries consequences not currently visible to
users or regulators.
