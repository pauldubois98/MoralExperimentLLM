TITLE
=====
Frontier LLMs Fall Outside Human Moral Space: A Replication and Extension of the
Moral Machine Experiment Across Seven Frontier Systems


ABSTRACT (plain text, for the FAccT submission form)
====================================================
The Moral Machine experiment collected over 40 million human judgments on
autonomous vehicle dilemmas across 130 countries, establishing the largest
empirical record of human moral preferences along nine dimensions. We replicate
that paradigm on seven frontier large language models (LLMs) released in 2025,
spanning six providers across the United States, Europe, and China, and comparing
27,300 model decisions against country-level human baselines using Average
Marginal Causal Effects (AMCEs).

All seven models fall outside the convex hull of all 130 countries in the
nine-dimensional AMCE space: no model's moral profile is matched by any human
population, and 47 of 63 model-by-dimension comparisons differ significantly from
the human average after FDR correction. Inter-model divergence substantially
exceeds cross-country human variation; on the species dimension the range across
models is roughly five times the entire cross-country human range. Two dimensions
on which humans are consistent are near-universally suppressed: all seven models
are effectively gender-neutral and status-neutral, against human preferences of
+0.12 and +0.35, a pattern consistent with fairness interventions targeting
stereotyped generation generalizing into moral reasoning that was never their
target.

We further introduce a systematic prompt sensitivity analysis. Minor
reformulations move no estimate significantly (0 of 63), while a single-sentence
ethical override reverses the species preference from +1.00 to -1.00 in four of
seven models, dividing systems into three response types: compliance,
indifference, and resistance. These results locate the moral profile of a deployed
LLM in provider training and alignment choices rather than in any human
population, making provider selection a consequential and currently undisclosed
moral decision, and they show that the resulting profile can be overridden by an
adversary controlling one line of context.


KEYWORDS
========
large language models; moral preferences; Moral Machine experiment; value
alignment; autonomous vehicles; prompt sensitivity; AI governance; model auditing
